The basics

What an ATS actually does with your resume

An applicant tracking system isn't an AI judge — it's a pipeline. Understanding the pipeline tells you exactly what to optimize.

1. Parse

The system extracts your text and sorts it into fields: name, contact, experience, education, skills. Anything it can't read — graphics, columns, images — is silently dropped.

2. Knock out

Hard filters remove resumes missing basics: no location, no degree where required, no work authorization. Formatting failures can look like missing data here.

3. Rank

Survivors get scored on keyword overlap with the job ad and other signals. Recruiters usually read only the top-ranked ones. This is where your keywords decide your fate.

Takeaway: you don't need to "trick" the ATS. You need to survive the parse, pass the knockout, and rank on keywords. Every rule below serves one of those three steps.

The rules

The 10 formatting rules parsers reward

1. One column only

Two-column layouts get read out of order — your skills can end up spliced into your job titles. Single column, top to bottom.

2. Standard headings

Use exactly "Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Projects". Creative headings like "My Journey" get ignored by the parser.

3. Real text, not images

Everything must be selectable text. Logos, signature images, and skill graphics are invisible to the parser.

4. Contact info in the body

Put your email and phone in the main text. Parsers often skip headers and footers entirely.

5. Mirror the job ad's keywords

Use the posting's exact terms for skills, tools, and titles. This is the single biggest ranking factor you control.

6. Plain bullets

Use simple bullet characters (• or -), one achievement per line. Fancy glyphs and nested tables break the read order.

7. Standard fonts

Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Times New Roman. Decorative fonts can fail to embed or render as gibberish in extracted text.

8. No tables or text boxes

Tables scramble the reading order and text boxes are often skipped. If you can see a grid, the parser is confused.

9. Keep it 300–1000 words

About one to two pages. Too short starves the keyword matcher; too long dilutes your keywords.

10. Quantify achievements

Numbers parse perfectly and rank well: "grew signups 32%", "cut load time 1.8s → 0.4s". Duties without results rank lowest.

The formula

Write bullets that score: action + result + number

Parsers and recruiters rank the same thing: proof of impact. Use this formula for every bullet — strong verb → what you did → measurable result.

❌ Weak: "Responsible for managing the company's social media accounts."

✅ Strong: "Grew Instagram following from 2k to 18k in 6 months, lifting profile-driven sales 24%."

❌ Weak: "Worked on machine learning projects at university."

✅ Strong: "Built a CNN image classifier (PyTorch) reaching 85% accuracy on a 12k-image dataset; presented to faculty panel."

❌ Weak: "Helped customers with their problems."

✅ Strong: "Resolved 40+ support tickets/week with a 96% satisfaction rating, cutting average response time from 6h to 2h."

Start every bullet with a strong verb — built, led, grew, cut, launched, automated — never "responsible for" or "helped with". One achievement per line, plain bullets, numbers wherever you honestly can.

Ranking

Keywords: mirror the job ad, don't stuff it

After formatting, keyword overlap is how most systems rank you. Here's the honest way to do it:

Copy the ad's exact terms

If the ad says "React" and "CI/CD", write exactly that — not "ReactJS" and "continuous deployment". Parsers match literal terms first.

Spread them naturally

The same keyword in your Skills list and inside an experience bullet scores better than repeating it 10 times in one block.

Match the top 8–15 terms

Focus on skills, tools, and job titles from the ad's requirements section. You don't need every word — you need the important ones.

Never keyword-stuff

White text, hidden blocks, and 30x repetition get flagged by modern systems — and horrify the human who eventually reads it.

Myths busted

4 things that don't help (and 1 that does)

❌ "A creative design stands out." It stands out to the parser as unreadable. Clean and scannable beats pretty every time at the screening stage.

❌ "ATS reads every PDF perfectly." Only text-based PDFs. A scanned or image PDF reads as a blank page — zero keywords, instant rejection.

❌ "More pages = more impressive." Keyword dilution is real. Two tight pages outrank four padded ones.

❌ "One resume fits all jobs." Tailoring your keywords to each posting is the highest-leverage 15 minutes in your job search.

✅ What actually works: clean single-column format + exact keywords from the ad + quantified bullets. Boring, proven, effective.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What does ATS-friendly mean?

It means a resume formatted so that applicant tracking system software can read it correctly: single-column layout, standard section headings (Experience, Education, Skills), real selectable text instead of images, and keywords that match the job ad. It's about being readable by software first, humans second.

Do I really need an ATS-friendly resume?

If you apply online to medium or large companies, yes — most run every resume through an ATS before a human ever looks. A resume the parser can't read correctly gets ranked low or filtered out, no matter how qualified you are.

Can I use a two-column resume template?

Not if you want it to pass an ATS. Two-column layouts, text boxes, and tables routinely get read out of order by parsers — your skills section can end up spliced into your work history. Use a single column.

Should I submit my resume as PDF or Word?

A text-based PDF is safe with most modern systems; Word (.docx) is the safest universally. Never submit a scanned or image-based PDF — parsers read it as a blank page. If in doubt, follow what the job posting asks for.

How many keywords should my resume have?

Mirror the job ad's most important 8–15 terms naturally — skills, tools, and job titles — in your experience bullets and skills section. Keyword stuffing gets flagged; natural repetition across relevant sections scores best.

Do graphics, icons, and photos hurt my ATS score?

Yes. Parsers can't read skill-bar graphics, icons, or photos — they see nothing where you think there's content. Write "Python (3 years)" instead of a bar chart. Photos can also trigger bias filters, so skip them unless the job asks.

How long should an ATS-friendly resume be?

Roughly 300–1000 words — about one to two pages. Too short starves the keyword matcher; much longer dilutes your keywords and bores the human who reads it next.

Does a high ATS score guarantee I'll pass?

No — every employer's system parses and ranks differently, and no format can fix weak content. What a high score means is that formatting is no longer the reason you're being filtered out, which puts you ahead of most applicants.
