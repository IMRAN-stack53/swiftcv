    The rules

    What the score checks

    Contact info parses

    A findable email and phone. Parsers skip headers and footers, so contact info must live in the body text.

    Standard sections

    Experience, Education, Skills — the exact headings parsers look for. Creative headings get ignored.

    Sensible length

    300–1000 words, roughly one to two pages. Too short looks empty; too long dilutes your keywords.

    Real bullets

    Plain bullet characters with one achievement per line — the format parsers read most reliably.

    Numbers & achievements

    Percentages and figures ("grew sales 32%") — parsers and recruiters both rank these higher than bare duties.

    Job-ad keywords

    After fixing format, mirror the posting's exact terms — keyword overlap is how most systems rank. Use our keyword matcher.

    How is the ATS score calculated?

    Your resume text is checked against 8 widely-agreed ATS rules: an email address present, a phone number present, an Experience section, an Education section, a Skills section, good length (300–1000 words), bullet points used, and numbers or achievements mentioned. Each check passes, warns, or fails, and the score is the weighted average out of 100.

    What is a good ATS score?

    Aim for 75 or above. Scores of 85+ (grade A) mean your resume follows the formatting rules parsers reward; 70–84 (B) is solid; below 50 means formatting issues are likely hurting your chances.

    Can I check a resume I made somewhere else?

    Does a high score guarantee my resume passes the ATS?

    No tool can guarantee that — every employer's system parses differently. What a high score means is that your resume follows every widely-agreed ATS best practice (single column, standard headings, real text, keyword-rich content), which puts you ahead of most applicants.
